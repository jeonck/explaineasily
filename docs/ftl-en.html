In one breath

The Flash Translation Layer (FTL) links a title (logical address) to its real slot (physical address), and writes any edit into a fresh slot instead of overwriting the old one.

The FTL is the layer inside an SSD that translates logical block addresses (LBAs) into physical NAND flash addresses. Flash can't be overwritten in place and can only be erased block-by-block, so a write marks the old page invalid and writes to a fresh page instead. Garbage collection later erases and reclaims whole blocks full of invalid pages, while wear leveling spreads writes evenly so no single block wears out faster than the rest.

When grown-ups say it

Flash Translation Layer
The shelf manager linking a title to its slot. Handles all of this inside the SSD.
Logical/Physical Address
The book's title vs. the slot's real number. FTL is what links the two together.
Garbage Collection
Gathering disposal slots and clearing them together. Can cause brief slowdowns while it runs.
Wear Leveling
Spreading use evenly across every slot. Prevents one slot from wearing out alone.
Over-provisioning
Slots kept empty in reserve, always. Protects both write speed and lifespan.
Block/Page
The big slot that clears all at once, and the small slots inside it. Erasing works by block; writing works by page.
Configuration Management
The shelf that keeps a manuscript's past versions. A similar "keep the old, write the new" idea shows up there too. → the shelf that protects the manuscript
High Availability
Holding up even when one slot fails. Covered in another world, not this one. → when the spare generator takes over
