What is IoT / OT Security?

IoT and OT security, told as a story about guarding the castle's silent working machines — the well pump, the gate pulley, and the thermometer.

The castle has many working machines, not just people.The well pump, the gate pulley, the thermometer. They work day and night, but they cannot talk. Who touched them, whether something is wrong — they cannot say.

You can't guard them the way you guard people.The guard dog can't get inside a machine. The password is still the one from the factory. The carpenter's plank won't fit, and if it stops once, the whole castle goes thirsty.

IoT and OT security is giving the machines their own hall, with a watcher who watches for them.The machines live in their own hall past the ribbon. Since they cannot speak for themselves, the hall watcher watches for them. And every machine goes on the list.

The watcher never touches the machine. It only listens from the hall.If a machine suddenly starts talking strangely, the hall watcher rings the bell — without stopping the machine. The thief can't cross the ribbon, and the factory password gets replaced.

The well keeps running, and the thief never reaches it.Because a machine must never stop, the order is different — first the ribbon, then the watcher, and planks only on a rest day. Which machines you have, count from outside too.

In one breath

IoT / OT security = keep the castle's silent machines in their own hall past the ribbon, let the watcher listen for them, put them all on the list, and change the factory password. Never stopped, never reached.

Protecting sensors, cameras, and industrial equipment that can't run an agent and are hard to patch. Network segmentation, passive monitoring, an asset inventory, and changing default passwords are the core — and availability and safety always come first.

When grown-ups say it

IoT
The small silent machines. Thermometers, cameras, door sensors. Connected to the village, but no room for a guard dog.
OT · ICS
The big machines that run the castle. The well pump, the gate pulley. Factory and power-plant equipment. If it stops, the castle stops.
Default password
The factory password. Like admin/admin, exactly as it left the factory. It's on the first page of every thief's book. → the password story
Firmware update
The plank for a machine. Hard, because the machine is old or can't be stopped. Done on a rest day. → the plank the carpenter sent
Network segmentation
The machine hall behind the ribbon. No crossing from the people's hall into the machines'. → a corridor split by colored ribbons
Passive monitoring
Listening without touching. The watcher only listens to the machines from the hall. → the one who guards the corridor
Legacy
The very old pulley. The smithy that made it is gone, so no plank exists. That's why the ribbon comes first. → counting the machines
Safety vs security
Not stopping comes first. In the people's castle, locking comes first; in the machines', the well running comes first. So you guard from outside.
