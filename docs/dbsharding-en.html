What is Database Sharding & Partitioning?

Database sharding and partitioning, told as a story about splitting an overgrown warehouse into zones.

So many boxes that finding one takes forever.One single warehouse can't keep up.

Splitting randomly piles everything into one zone.The splitting key has to be chosen carefully.

Sharding splits the warehouse into several zones (servers).Each zone only handles its own share of boxes.

Sharding splits servers; partitioning splits zones within one warehouse.Both hinge on choosing the right split key.

Splitting helps speed, but cross-zone questions get slower.Especially true for queries that need a join.

In one breath

Sharding/partitioning splits an overgrown warehouse into zones (or servers), each responsible for its own share.

Sharding horizontally splits data across multiple physical servers (shards) for scalability. Partitioning splits a table horizontally or vertically within one server/DB. Both pay a cost in performance and consistency when a join or transaction spans more than one shard or partition.

When grown-ups say it

Sharding
Splitting across several servers (warehouses). Choosing the shard key matters most.
Partitioning
Splitting zones within one warehouse. Can go horizontal or vertical.
Shard Key
The tag deciding which zone it goes to. Pick it wrong and one zone gets overloaded.
Join Cost
The work of searching every zone. Grows as sharding gets more fine-grained.
Sharding (Distributed Systems View)
The deeper story from the amusement-park world. Replication, consensus, and CAP are covered there in depth. → sharding in distributed systems
Concurrency Control
Rules that stop simultaneous edits from colliding. Still needed inside each zone. → the no-collision rule
NoSQL Modeling
The free-shelf warehouse. Often makes sharding easier. → the free-shelf warehouse
Normalization
Writing shared info on one shelf once. Too much normalization means more joins, clashing with sharding. → the rule of writing it once
