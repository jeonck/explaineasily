In one breath

Hardware sizing = a formula sheet that multiplies expected headcount and usage by peak load, safety margin, and growth rate, calculated in advance.

The IS hardware sizing guideline is an administrative standard that calculates server and storage capacity with formulas based on expected users, transaction throughput (TPS), and data growth rate. It sizes for peak load rather than average, and builds in a safety margin plus future expansion.

When grown-ups say it

Capacity Sizing
Measuring the needed amount in advance with a formula. Leaves a numeric basis, not just a guess.
TPS (Transactions Per Second)
Orders handled per second. The basic unit for measuring server performance.
Peak Load
The amount at the busiest moment. Sizing by average falls short at the busiest moment.
Safety Margin
The extra percentage added instead of cutting it exact. Lets the system hold up even if demand suddenly rises.
Growth Projection
Building in next year's expected increase. Because what's bought has to last several years.
Availability
How much it must keep running without stopping. Another world covers this alongside sizing. → the ticket that guarantees the show
Capacity Planning
Planning how much more will be needed ahead of time. A similar plan exists in another world too. → planning more seats ahead of time
Benchmark Test
Testing how much it can actually handle. Not trusting the formula alone — testing it for real.
