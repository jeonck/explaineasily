What is the Three Data Acts Amendment?

The 2020 Three Data Acts amendment, told as a story about gathering three separately-run rulebooks under one inspector, and inventing one new label.

In one breath

The Three Data Acts amendment gathered three separately-run rulebooks — the Personal Information Protection Act, the Network Act, the Credit Information Act — under one inspector (the Personal Information Protection Commission), and invented a new label: pseudonymized data. A 2020 legal reform.

The 2020 Three Data Acts amendment. It realigned the personal-data provisions of the Personal Information Protection Act, the Act on Promotion of Information and Communications Network Utilization and Information Protection (the Network Act), and the Credit Information Act, and unified oversight under the Personal Information Protection Commission. Its core change was introducing 'pseudonymized data' — information processed so a specific individual can't be identified without additional information — which may be processed without the data subject's consent for statistics, research, or public-interest record-keeping. The three Acts themselves were not literally merged; each still exists as a separate law.

When grown-ups say it

Three Data Acts
The castle's three rulebooks. The collective name for the Personal Information Protection Act, the Network Act, and the Credit Information Act.
Personal Information Protection Commission
The one inspector watching all three rulebooks. The 2020 amendment gathered oversight authority here.
Pseudonymized data
A guest record with the name removed. Processed so a specific person can't be identified without extra information.
Anonymized data
A record scrubbed so it can never be traced back. Falls outside the scope of this law entirely.
Re-identification ban
Forbidding tracing pseudonymized data back to the original person. The core condition attached to processing pseudonymized data.
Personal Information Protection Act
The most foundational of the three rulebooks. The broad framework for protecting personal data. → the visitor's name tag belongs to the visitor
Credit Information Act
The rulebook that opened financial MyData first. MyData's legal basis traces back to this Act. → my right to carry my own ledger
Compliance
The inspector confirming things follow the rulebook. Whether the Three Data Acts are followed is checked through this same frame. → the inspector from the neighboring kingdom
