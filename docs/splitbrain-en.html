In one breath

Split-brain = two disconnected control rooms, each believing it alone is in charge, giving conflicting orders at the same time. Prevented by keeping an odd number of rooms and recognizing only a majority.

A state where two parts of a distributed system each believe they're the leader, caused by a network partition. Requiring a quorum (a majority) prevents it, since neither side alone can reach one and both pause. Forcibly shutting down a fake leader is called fencing.

When grown-ups say it

Split-brain
Two control rooms each insisting they're the real one. Conflicting orders throw the park out of sync.
Network partition
The line between control rooms going down. This is what causes split-brain.
Quorum
The majority a side must clear to be recognized as on call. Fail to reach it, and a side pauses itself. → control rooms raising hands to decide
Fencing
Forcibly shutting a fake leader down. Done during cleanup.
Odd node count
Keeping control rooms at an odd number, like 3 or 5. It keeps the majority from ever being ambiguous.
Safe mode
Pausing itself when it can't reach a majority. It stops before conflicting orders go out.
Leader election
The vote that re-picks a leader after recovery. → control rooms raising hands to decide
Multi-region
The same risk applies when control rooms sit in different cities. A design meant to survive losing a whole city.
