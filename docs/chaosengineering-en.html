What is Chaos Engineering?

Chaos engineering, told as a story about quietly checking whether the spare generator actually works.

Does our spare generator actually work?No one's sure — it's never been switched off.

A safety net you've never tested might fail when you need it most.It's just gathering dust — no one even remembers when it was last turned on.

Secretly switch one ride off during normal hours — if the spare takes right over, it worked.Do it small, while real guests are still there.

Cause a small break on purposejust one thing
Do it during real operationnot a test park
Start small, then grow itwiden it bit by bit
Be ready to stop instantlyso you can undo it

A mechanic quietly switches one off, then checks the gauge to see if the spare takes over.Power drops for a moment, then climbs back once the spare kicks in.

Doing it with no plan at all causes a real accident.It has to be small, reversible, and announced ahead of time.

In one breath

Chaos engineering = deliberately causing a small, controlled break during normal operation to check whether your backups actually work.

Chaos engineering deliberately injects failure into a system to confirm it holds up the way it was designed to. You form a hypothesis ("it should survive this"), keep the blast radius small, and widen the scope gradually in the real production environment. Doing it on a regular schedule becomes a game day.

When grown-ups say it

Chaos Engineering
Quietly checking whether your backup actually works. Done on purpose, small, and planned in advance.
Fault injection
Deliberately switching one ride off. This is the actual act chaos engineering performs.
A planned experiment
Not random — an experiment planned ahead of time. Decide in advance when, what, and how long.
Hypothesis testing
Confirming the belief that "it should survive this." If you're wrong, that's the real payoff.
Limiting the blast radius
Starting small and widening it gradually. You never switch off the whole park at once.
Relation to game days
A scheduled drill everyone does together. Doing it on a regular schedule becomes a game day. → a fake fire drill day
Failover
The very act of the spare taking over. Chaos engineering is how you confirm this actually works.
Resilience verification
Turning "it should survive" into "it did survive." Different from a promise made only in words. → the people who keep the park open
