What is a Log?

Logs, told as a story about the one-line diary kept at every door in the castle.

Things happen in the castle and just pass.Who opened which door, and when — once it's over, nobody knows.

When something goes wrong, there's nobody to ask."Who was in the vault last night?" — nobody knows.

A log is the one-line record kept all over the castle.'When, who, where, what' — one line at a time. Every door, every counter, every thing.

Gather the lines in time order, and they become a story.Alone, one line; together, the thief's trail. The big screen gathers and lines them up. Months of them go in a drawer.

Unwritten lines don't exist, thieves tear pages, and too many can't be read.A door writes only if it's told to. Thieves tear the diary first — so a copy goes far away. And thousands a day are beyond any person — the guard room's big screen reads them instead.

In one breath

A log = the one-line record — when, who, where, what — kept all over the castle. Gathered in time order, it becomes a story.

The first ingredient of any investigation. Four jobs: write it (configure), gather it (SIEM), protect it (send it away), keep it (retention). Start by setting the clocks.

When grown-ups say it

Event
One line. One thing that happened: a door opened, a letter came.
Timestamp
When. Every clock in the castle must agree, or nothing lines up (NTP).
Source
Where. Which door, which counter, which thing wrote it.
Audit log
Who changed what. Took a key, swapped a hat. → the key ledger
Access log
The list of counter visitors. Who handed in which note, when. → the note checker
Log forwarding
Sending a copy far away. Tear the diary and the copy survives. → the big screen
Retention
Months in the drawer. To find footprints later, keep them a long while. → the spare chest
Log tampering
Tearing the diary. A thief erasing only their own lines. Copies and stamps (hashes) prevent it.
