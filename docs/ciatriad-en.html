What is the CIA Triad?

The CIA triad — confidentiality, integrity, availability — told as a story about the three promises a castle keeps.

A castle guards against more than thieves.Even with thieves out, someone can peek at a letter, quietly change the ledger, or block the gate. Three different kinds of trouble.

Keep just one promise, and the castle stops.The guard locked everything in the vault and lost the key. Nobody can peek — not even the cook. So there is no bread today.

The CIA triad is the three promises a castle keeps.No peeking (sealed letters, colored stamps), no tampering (fingerprints, the king's seal), always there (spare chests). All three, together.

No peekingstolen, still unreadable
No tamperingchange it, the print won't match
Always therethrough fires and fake crowds
All three at oncepush one too hard, another falls

Every tool in the castle keeps one of the three.Key rings keep no peeking, the logbook keeps no tampering, line ushers and the next-day plan keep always there. Meet a new tool, and just ask: which of the three?

The three pull on each other. Every castle sets its own balance.Tighter, and nobody can use it; looser, and it leaks. Where to lean is decided on the scale, and whatever you keep, you keep in layers.

In one breath

The CIA triad = the three promises a castle keeps — no peeking, no tampering, always there. They pull on each other, so you weigh them together.

Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability — the three goals of information security. Encryption and access control serve confidentiality; hashing, signatures, and logs serve integrity; backups, redundancy, and DDoS defense serve availability. They trade off, so the balance follows the risk.

When grown-ups say it

Confidentiality
No peeking. Only those allowed get to see. → the letter no one reads without the key
Integrity
No tampering. If it changes in secret, you can tell. → a fingerprint on everything
Availability
Always there. It has to be there when needed. → the spare chest far away, fake guests at the gate
Non-repudiation
No saying you never sent it. The king's seal is on it. → the badge with the king's seal
Authentication
Proving you are you. To keep any promise, you first need to know who is asking. → the doorkeeper who checks three times
Trade-off
The three-way scale. Tighter means unusable, looser means leaky. A bank leans toward no tampering, a notice board toward always there.
Risk
Which way to lean. Keep first the promise that hurts most to break. → the scale that picks which door to guard first
Defense in depth
In layers. No promise rests on a single tool. → walls within walls
