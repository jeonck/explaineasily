In one breath

Ensemble methods pool the calls of several models into a majority vote or average, on the idea that the result is steadier than any single model.

Ensembles combine multiple weak learners into a stronger predictor. Bagging trains models independently on bootstrap samples and averages or votes their outputs (e.g. Random Forest); boosting trains models sequentially, each weighting the previous model's misses more heavily (e.g. AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting). Bagging mainly reduces variance and boosting mainly reduces bias, but both cost more compute and are harder to explain than a single model.

When grown-ups say it

Bagging
Splitting the crops differently for each operator. Random Forest is the classic example of bagging.
Boosting
The next operator focusing on what the last one got wrong. AdaBoost and Gradient Boosting are classic examples.
Voting
Picking whichever answer came up most. Can be plain majority or a weighted average.
Random Forest
The classic bagging method that grows many sorters at once. Built from pooling many decision trees.
Bias-Variance Tradeoff
The tug-of-war between no tilt and no swing. Bagging mainly tames variance; boosting mainly tames bias.
