What is Deadlock and Starvation?

Deadlock and starvation, told as a story about two mechanics each holding a key the other needs.

In one breath

Deadlock = freezing forever as each side holds one resource (key) and waits on another. Starvation isn't frozen — one side just never gets its turn.

Deadlock arises when four conditions hold at once: Mutual Exclusion, Hold and Wait, No Preemption, and Circular Wait. Fixes include prevention (design away one condition), avoidance (allow only safe states, as in the banker's algorithm), detection-and-recovery, or simply ignoring it (the ostrich algorithm). Starvation differs from deadlock: the system keeps running, but one process never gets the resource it needs because of scheduling or priority.

When grown-ups say it

Mutual Exclusion
One key, one holder at a time. One of the four conditions.
Circular Wait
Everyone waiting in a loop for the next one's key. Break this loop, and deadlock breaks too.
Starvation
One car keeps getting bumped down the order. Not a freeze — an endless postponement.
CPU Scheduling
The rule that sets the car order on the board. A priority rule can itself cause starvation. → who gets the lift first
Synchronization
The rule for handing out the lift key. Deadlock often comes from misusing these tools. → there's only one lift key
Process/Thread
Several hands working the same car. Deadlock can happen between threads or between processes. → one car, many hands
Virtual Memory
A fake map that makes the whole warehouse look like mine. Another example of sharing a limited resource. → the fake map that makes the whole warehouse yours
Orchestration
The amusement-park world's version of ordering work. Distributed systems handle resource deadlock in a similar spirit. → orchestration in the SRE world
