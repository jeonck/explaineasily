In one breath

Net neutrality = the rule that everyone's data crossing the hallway (the network) is treated the same, with no discrimination, blocking, or cutting in line.

Net neutrality is the principle that internet service providers must not discriminate against or block traffic based on its content, source, or type of service. Not all traffic management is banned, though — only 'unreasonable discrimination' is regulated, while reasonable prioritization for things like emergency or medical traffic can still be allowed.

When grown-ups say it

Network Neutrality
The rule that stops cutting in line. Stops a carrier from favoring one kind of traffic.
Traffic Discrimination
Speeding up or slowing down one service on purpose. The core behavior net neutrality is meant to stop.
Zero-Rating
Not charging data fees for one specific app. Looks convenient, but it's a frequent flashpoint in the debate.
Traffic Management
Adjusting flow when things get congested. Reasonable management is allowed — as long as it isn't discrimination.
Net Neutrality vs Network-Use Fees
The line-order rule and who pays for the line are different issues. The fee debate is a separate question from net neutrality.
Transparency Obligation
Publishing how traffic is managed instead of hiding it. Lets anyone check whether a discrimination is unreasonable.
Reasonable Traffic Management
Management everyone can accept, like congestion control. Distinguished from unreasonable discrimination.
Platform Regulation
A different rule for when one app grows too dominant. Shares a similar fairness tension. → the one app that does everything
