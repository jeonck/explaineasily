In one breath

On-call means one person carries the pager for the week — first to see it, day or night, rotating week by week, and escalating to the next person if there's no answer.

The rotation of people who get paged when something breaks. Too many alerts cause alert fatigue, which can bury the real emergency — so teams choose carefully what triggers a page and leave handover notes at every switch.

When grown-ups say it

On-call
Whoever's holding the pager this week. First to be paged when something breaks.
Rotation schedule
The calendar that rotates week by week. Shows at a glance whose week it is.
Escalation
Passing it to the next person when there's no answer. It's fine if one person can't always answer.
Alert fatigue
Being worn out by too many alerts. It makes the real emergency get missed. → how many rings mean a real emergency
Handover notes
The notes passed along at the switch. Tells the next person what happened last week.
On-call compensation
What's paid back for carrying the pager. Often extra pay or time off in return.
Paging
The device that rings to call someone. Covers texts, calls, and app alerts alike.
Incident commander
Whoever takes charge when it's a big incident. On-call hands it off if it grows bigger. → whoever picks up the baton
