What is a Distributed Database?

A distributed database, told as a story about splitting one giant warehouse into regional branches that keep each other in sync. The deeper distributed-systems theory lives in the SRE world.

Every order nationwide piles onto one single warehouse.A far-away warehouse means slow delivery too.

In one breath

A distributed database stores one logical database split across many nodes, using replication and consensus to make it look like a single database.

From a DB-modeling exam angle, this is about choosing a sharding key, picking sync vs. async replication, and the rising cost of cross-node joins and transactions. The deeper distributed-systems theory — CAP theorem, consensus algorithms — is covered in more depth over in the SRE world.

When grown-ups say it

Sharding
Splitting goods by branch. Choosing the split key is covered right here. → splitting an overgrown warehouse into zones
Replication
Keeping a duplicate box at each branch. Covers sync vs. async replication. → the world of duplicate boxes
Consensus
Several branches voting on one answer. Covers algorithms like Raft/Paxos. → deciding by a vote among many
CAP Theorem
You can't have all three perfectly. The consistency/availability/partition trade-off. → the law you can't have all three of at once
Eventual Consistency
Eventually matching up, even if a bit late. Numbers can differ for a moment. → the story of eventually matching up
Data Locality
Handling it at the nearest branch. The closer it is, the faster the answer.
Distributed Transaction (2PC)
A transaction slip spanning several branches. It only finalizes once every branch agrees.
NoSQL
A warehouse without fixed shelf slots. Loosens the structure to make distribution easier.
