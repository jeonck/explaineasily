What is Quantitative Risk Analysis?

Quantitative risk analysis, told as a story about a safety officer who multiplies probability by impact and writes risk as one number on a scoreboard.

Every safety officer says something different when a booth 'looks risky.''Looks risky' means something different to every person.

The same booth gets different verdicts from different officers.When the same booth gets different verdicts, you can't tell what to fix first.

Quantitative risk analysis multiplies probability by impact, writing risk as one single number.A number instead of a feeling — so anyone can rank the risks the same way.

Measure probabilityhow often might it happen
Measure impacthow big would the damage be
Multiply for a scoreprobability × impact
Rank by scorefix the highest number first

Risk comes from multiplying probability by impact.Formulas like ALE (annual loss expectancy) refine this idea further.

Guess the probability wrong, and the number is wrong too.The number looks precise, but a human guess still sits underneath it.

In one breath

Quantitative risk analysis = calculating probability × impact to compare the size of risks as a single number.

Quantitative risk analysis scores a risk as probability multiplied by loss magnitude, letting different risks be compared and prioritized objectively. ALE (Annual Loss Expectancy), ROSI (Return on Security Investment), and risk matrices are common tools, typically paired with qualitative analysis (high/medium/low judgment calls) as a complement.

When grown-ups say it

Annual Loss Expectancy
Adding up a year's worth of expected damage in advance. It's probability times impact, scaled to a year.
Risk Matrix
Placing probability and impact into one grid cell. A way of showing the same idea with colored cells instead of numbers.
Qualitative Risk Analysis
Judging risk only as high, medium, or low. A complementary method that pairs with the quantitative one.
Return on Security Investment
A calculation of whether security spending pays for itself. It feeds the quantitative risk number into a spending decision.
