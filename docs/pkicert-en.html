What are Accredited and Private Certificates?

Accredited (공인) vs. private (사설) certificates, told as a story comparing the days of one state-designated seal-shop with today's many.

In one breath

There used to be only one seal-shop with legal effect — the accredited certificate — but the 2020 amendment to the Digital Signature Act ended that privilege, giving several private certificates equal legal standing.

After the 1999 Digital Signature Act, only government-designated accredited certificate authorities could issue a 공인인증서, which carried a stronger legal presumption than private electronic signatures. The 2020 full revision of the Act abolished that special status; the old certificate survives as the renamed '공동인증서' (Joint Certificate), now standing on equal legal footing with other private certificates like KakaoPay, PASS, and Naver. Technically, both accredited and private certificates run on PKI (Public Key Infrastructure) — what changed was the legal status, not the cryptography.

When grown-ups say it

Accredited certificate
Once the only seal-shop with legal weight. Renamed '공동인증서' (Joint Certificate) after the 2020 amendment.
Private certificate
The new, other seal-shops. KakaoPay, PASS, Naver, and more — now carrying equal legal effect.
Digital Signature Act
The law that decides who may run a seal-shop. The 2020 full revision ended the accredited certificate's special status.
Joint certificate
The old accredited certificate's new name. The privilege is gone, but it's still one option among many.
PKI
The actual mechanism for stamping and checking the seal. Both accredited and private certificates run on this. → the badge with the king's seal
Digital signature
The king's seal stamped at the end of a letter. Legally carries the same weight as a handwritten signature.
Simple authentication
Identity verification done with just one app. Commonly seen across MyData and financial services. → my right to carry my own ledger
