What is Spyware?

Spyware, adware and cryptojacking, told as a story about the three quiet cousins of the bug in the gift box.

Something is off in the castle. It's slow, flyers cover the windows, and the candle burns down fast.Nobody locked a chest and no bell rang. And yet something about the castle is wrong.

The lock bug is loud. These bugs have to stay quiet to stay long.The lock bug gets noticed at once — being noticed is the point. The cousins are the opposite. The day they're noticed, their business is over.

The bug's three cousins — the spying bug, the ad bug, and the bug that makes you work.All three are family to the bug in the gift box. They break nothing, lock nothing, and quietly take what is yours.

The spying bugwrites down passwords, letters, what's out the window
The ad bugsticks flyers on windows and gets paid
The working bugspins our wheel all night to make its own gold
All three stay quietgetting noticed is the end for them

The way to notice is the candle. When a bug turns the wheel, the candle burns fast.The candle is electricity and the computer's power. Slow, hot, and a bigger bill — be suspicious. Then the guard dog and the bug cards find it.

Most ride in with a box of free tools. Sometimes someone you know plants one.Open an unknown box in the windowless empty room first. If there's a bug, it can only crawl around in there.

In one breath

Spyware, adware, cryptojacking = the bug's three quiet cousins. One writes down your passwords, one sticks flyers on every window, one turns your wheel all night to make its own gold. A slow castle and a fast-burning candle are the giveaway.

Spyware secretly collects keystrokes (keyloggers), screens and files and sends them out; adware injects ads for profit; cryptojacking mines cryptocurrency on someone else's CPU and electricity. All three aim to stay unnoticed, so watch CPU usage, network traffic, and what is installed.

When grown-ups say it

Spyware
Watches in secret, sends in secret. Passwords, letters, where you go. Sent to the thief outside the castle every night.
Keylogger
Writes down the password you say at the door. Every letter, one by one. That's why a password alone isn't enough → the doorkeeper who checks three times
Adware
Flyers on every window. The bug's owner gets paid for every flyer. The mildest cousin — but annoying, and it slows things down.
Cryptojacking
Turns our wheel all night. Makes its own gold (cryptocurrency) with our power and electricity. The reason the candle burns fast.
Stalkerware
Planted by family or a partner. The same as the spying bug, but planted by someone you know. Common on phones.
Bundled install
Tucked in the corner of a toolbox. Free tools come with a bug attached. Look inside the box before clicking next, next, next.
Ransomware vs. spyware
Loud vs. quiet. The lock bug earns by being noticed; the cousins earn by not being. → the thief's lock on every chest
Sandbox
The room for opening unknown boxes. If a bug comes out, it can't leave that room. → the windowless empty room
