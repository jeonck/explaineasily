In one breath

Coupling measures how tangled chapters (modules) are with each other; cohesion measures how unified a single chapter is inside. Good design aims for low coupling and high cohesion.

Coupling is the degree of interdependence between modules — the lower it is, the less a change in one module ripples into others. Cohesion is how focused the elements inside one module are on a single responsibility — the higher it is, the easier the module is to understand and reuse. Low coupling and high cohesion is a foundational principle of good software design.

When grown-ups say it

Coupling
How entangled chapters are with each other. Lower is easier to change.
Cohesion
How unified a chapter is around one topic. Higher is easier to understand.
Modularity
The act of splitting a book into chapters at all. The premise that makes coupling and cohesion meaningful.
Interface
A chapter's "entrance" — its title or table-of-contents entry. Hides the details, exposes only this.
Information Hiding
The principle of showing only what's needed and hiding the rest. A concrete technique for lowering coupling.
Product Line Engineering
Core assets need to be split well for reuse to work. Directly used when designing core assets. → same template, different covers
Conditions of Good Software
Most directly tied to maintainability. The principle underpinning one of those conditions. → what makes a good book
Design Pattern
A ready-made solution for achieving low coupling and high cohesion. A frequent entry in the style guide.
