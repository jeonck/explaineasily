What is Pricing?

LLM pricing (per-token billing), told as a story about a parrot that is priced per bean, not per visit.

The month-end bill is ten times the guess. And nobody knows what cost so much.The parrot only answered as told. Nobody counted where the beans leaked away.

The parrot is priced per bean, not per visit.Beans going in and beans coming out both cost. What a bean is: the parrot counts words in beans.

In one breath

Pricing = beans in × rate + beans out × rate. Beans out cost 3 to 5 times more, and a bigger parrot costs more per bean. Keep the tray short, cap the output, cache the same start, send easy jobs to the small parrot.

LLM APIs bill input tokens and output tokens at separate rates that vary by model tier (output is usually 3 to 5 times more). Prompt-cache hits earn a large discount, and batch APIs are the slow-but-cheap lane. Context length, output length, and model choice drive cost; agents and reasoning models use so many tokens that totals can rise even as rates fall.

When grown-ups say it

Token rate
The price of one bean. Usually listed per million beans. It differs by parrot size.
Input · output tokens
Beans in · beans out. Beans out cost 3 to 5 times more. Long answers add up fast.
Cache discount
Beans laid out ahead are cheap. Reusing the same start costs about a tenth. → beans laid out ahead
Context length cost
A bigger tray costs more. Every bean on the tray is charged every time. → the tray in front of the parrot
Model tier
Parrot size. A big parrot can cost ten times more per bean. Match it to the job.
Batch API
The slow but cheap lane. Gather questions that can wait, and they get answered at half price.
Budget cap
A lid on the bean sack. Only this much a month — set it to stop before it runs over.
Routing
Which parrot gets the job. Easy jobs to the small parrot, hard ones only to the big. → which parrot gets the job
