In one breath

Wireless charging technology lets electricity cross the air just by parking on a pad — no cable plugged in.

Wireless charging transfers power through magnetic induction (two coils close together) or magnetic resonance (matched resonant frequencies, working over a slightly longer distance). It's less efficient than a wired connection, but safer and more convenient since there's no exposed contact — used for EVs, robots, and mobile devices.

When grown-ups say it

Wireless charging
Charging that just needs parking on a pad, no cable. Contactless, so it's safer too.
Magnetic induction
Power crossing between two coils held close together. The shorter the distance, the better the efficiency.
Magnetic resonance
A way that works even a bit apart, by matching frequency. More tolerant of distance than plain induction.
5G Private Network
A delivery network dedicated to one factory. Often paired with wirelessly charged robots inside a factory. → a delivery network just for one factory
Qi standard
The shared pad spec many makers agree to use. Even different trucks can charge on the same pad.
Efficiency loss
The part of the power that scatters while crossing. Grows larger with distance or misalignment.
Charging coil
The part inside the pad that hands off the electricity. The truck needs a matching coil of its own too.
BMS
The manager who safely parcels out the power received. Prevents overcharging whether the power came wired or wireless.
