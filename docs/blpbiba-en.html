What are BLP and Biba?

The BLP and Biba access-control security models, told as a story about two rules that block climbing a rank ladder in opposite directions.

Every room in the castle carries its own secrecy rank.Under MAC (Mandatory Access Control), nobody can change this ranking at will.

Climb the ladder either way with no rule, and both end badly.Peek up, and it leaks. Write down, and it leaks. Either way, the secret flows downward.

BLP and Biba are two rules, paired as mirror images.BLP (1973) protects confidentiality; Biba (1977) protects integrity. Both are rules on the very same rank ladder.

BLP: no read upa low rank can't read a higher secret
BLP: no write downa high rank can't write into a low ledger
Biba: the exact mirrorno read down + no write up
A frequent exam topicalmost always asked as the difference between them

Side by side, here are the four rules.BLP blocks a secret leaking downward; Biba blocks something untrustworthy creeping upward.

Strict as they are, they're usually blended with other models in practice.These are the concrete rules behind MAC. Most company systems lean more on RBAC instead.

In one breath

BLP keeps a secret from leaking by letting nobody read up or write down the rank ladder; Biba mirrors it exactly, letting nobody read down or write up, to keep clean records clean.

Bell-LaPadula (BLP, 1973) is a confidentiality-focused MAC model enforcing No Read Up / No Write Down. Biba (1977) is an integrity-focused model enforcing No Read Down / No Write Up, so a lower-trust subject can never corrupt higher-ranked data. Both are the concrete, formal mathematical rules behind label-based Mandatory Access Control (MAC), originating in military and government classified systems.
