What is PPDM?

Privacy-Preserving Data Mining (PPDM), told as a story about a statistician who blurs each person while still reading the village's overall trend.

The statistician wants to know the village's overall trend.The ledger spells out every single villager's name and income in full.

Erase just the name, and a few remaining clues can still find the person again.Just erasing the name (masking) isn't enough. Age, neighborhood, and job alone can narrow it down to one person.

PPDM is a bundle of techniques that blur the individual while keeping the trend visible.A technique like homomorphic encryption — computing while still sealed — is one of PPDM's tools too.

Bundle k people togetherat least k people are made to look alike
Mix in a little noisedifferential privacy — nudges each value a bit
Compute while still sealedtechniques like homomorphic encryption
Export only the statisticno single person's row ever leaves

Each technique trades off something different.Homomorphic encryption is one of the tools PPDM reaches for. The stronger the protection, the less useful the statistic becomes.

Protection and usefulness always sit on the same scale.The risk of re-identification never hits zero. → the same worry behind pseudonymized data and privacy, applied to data analysis.

In one breath

PPDM = a bundle of techniques that blur each individual while still letting you read the village's overall trend (the statistics).

Privacy-Preserving Data Mining (PPDM) is an umbrella term for methods that extract statistical patterns while protecting individuals, instead of analyzing identifiable raw data directly — k-anonymity (bundling entities sharing attributes into groups of at least k), differential privacy (adding mathematically-controlled noise to results), and homomorphic encryption (computing directly on ciphertext) among them. Protection strength and analytical accuracy trade off against each other.

When grown-ups say it

Privacy-Preserving Data Mining
A bundle of techniques that blur the person but keep the trend visible. An umbrella name covering several techniques.
k-anonymity
Bundling so at least k people look identical. Prevents narrowing a record down to just one person.
Differential privacy
Adding mathematically-controlled noise to a value. Blurs the individual value while the overall statistic stays nearly intact.
Re-identification
Finding the erased name back again. Possible from just a few remaining clues. → a number tag instead of a name
Homomorphic encryption
A way to compute while things stay sealed. One of the tools PPDM reaches for. → the sealed letter you can do math on
Pseudonymized data
A record with the name removed. PPDM is especially used when mining statistics from this kind of data. → the Three Data Acts amendment
Accuracy trade-off
The stronger the protection, the less accurate it gets. You can't have perfect protection and perfect accuracy at the same time.
Privacy
The very thing being protected. PPDM is the technical way to keep this promise for statistics and research. → the visitor's name tag belongs to the visitor
