What is Blue-Green Deployment?

Blue-green deployment, told as a story about two identical rides, taking turns.

While fixing the ride, no guests can get on.For as long as the repair takes, the ride simply has to stay closed.

You can't keep letting guests ride something that's mid-repair.Fixing it while guests are still riding is even more dangerous.

Twin rides, running one at a time.While one takes guests, the other gets fully repaired.

Keep two identical ridesbuilt as twins from the start
Only one takes gueststhe other stays idle
Fully repair the idle oneslowly, and properly
Flip the arrow, and you're switchedalmost no downtime

Flip the sign, and the switch is done.The line that went to green now heads to blue instead.

Keeping two rides running costs twice as much.Switching back is easy — but the guest-log gap that built up in between needs care.

In one breath

Blue-green deployment = keeping two identical rides, fully repairing the idle one while the other keeps taking guests, then switching with no downtime by just flipping the sign.

A deployment strategy that prepares a whole second environment identical to the live one, tests it fully, and then switches all traffic over at once. If something goes wrong, flipping the arrow back reverts it instantly.

When grown-ups say it

Blue-green deployment
Running twin rides, one at a time. One serves, one's repaired — the roles swap.
Zero-downtime deployment
A deployment with almost no downtime. Guests barely notice the switch happening.
Switchover
The moment the sign gets flipped. One flip sends every guest to the new side.
Instant rollback
Flipping the sign back to where it was. Reverting is much simpler than with a canary
vs. canary
Switching everything, versus showing it to a few first. Canary starts at 1% and grows from there
Downtime
Time when no guests can get on. Blue-green cuts this down to almost nothing.
Twin-environment cost
The cost of keeping two rides running. It always takes double the space and staff.
