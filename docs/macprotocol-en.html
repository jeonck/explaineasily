In one breath

Multiple access control (MAC) is the set of rules that let several users share one communication medium without colliding (CSMA, TDMA, CDMA).

CSMA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access) senses whether the medium is idle before transmitting, and detects or avoids collisions (CSMA/CD, CSMA/CA). TDMA (Time Division) slices time into slots assigned per user. CDMA (Code Division) gives each user a unique spreading code, letting them share the same time and frequency simultaneously while still being told apart.

When grown-ups say it

CSMA
Listening whether the lane is clear before sending. Detects or avoids collisions when they happen.
TDMA
Splitting time and taking turns. Each carrier sends only in its own assigned slot.
CDMA
Sending at once, each with its own code. The receiver filters using only its own code.
CSMA/CD
Noticing that a collision happened. On collision, wait briefly and resend.
CSMA/CA
Avoiding the collision before it happens. Used where sensing a collision directly is hard, like wireless.
TCP/UDP
The registered envelope and the plain one. The envelopes used on top of whatever lane-sharing rule wins. → the registered envelope and the plain one
Network slicing
Splitting one road into dedicated lanes. Pre-splitting lanes cuts down collisions to begin with. → splitting the road into lanes
Wireless positioning
Finding your position from several signal towers. Positioning signals follow the same sharing rules too. → finding a position from three towers
