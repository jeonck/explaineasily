In one breath

The AI Basic Act = the legal floor every station must clear, with high-impact AI carrying heavier duties on top.

Korea's AI Basic Act imposes baseline duties of transparency and safety on every AI business, and separately classifies AI used in domains where life, bodily safety, or basic rights are heavily at stake — medicine, hiring, credit, the judiciary, and the like — as 'high-impact AI,' adding duties like advance notice, risk assessment, safety measures, and human oversight. The law only draws the floor; the detailed principles above it (AI ethics and governance) and the execution procedure (an AI risk management framework) are still something an organization has to build for itself.

When grown-ups say it

AI Basic Act
The minimum grading rule the country itself sets. Applies to every station alike.
High-impact AI
AI that heavily affects life, safety, or basic rights. Carries extra duties on top of the basics.
Advance notice duty
Telling people up front that AI is making the call. Lets the customer know and choose with open eyes.
Risk assessment duty
An assessment procedure added for high-impact AI. The same work as stages ② and ③ of the operations map. → The Operations Map That Walks the Whole Station
Safety assurance measures
Actual safeguards put in place to prevent accidents. Principle alone isn't enough — execution has to follow.
Human oversight
The mechanism that keeps a machine from finishing the call alone. Shares its roots with the human review the ethics charter guarantees. → The Grading Charter Every Station Shares
Legal floor
The minimum level the law guarantees. What's built above it is up to each organization's own ethics and risk management.
Fines & penalties
The legal consequence of failing these duties. What makes the law a real requirement, not just a suggestion.
