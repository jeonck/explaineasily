What is a Firewall?

Firewalls, told as a story about a wall with many doors and the gatekeeper who holds the door list.

The wall has more than one door.A guest door, a mail door, a storage door, an admin door… each has a number. That's a port.

With every door open, there's no wall at all.Thieves look for the back door nobody watches — an open port — first.

A firewall is the gatekeeper who opens and shuts doors by a list.'Guest door: anyone. Mail door: post office only. The rest: closed.' Not on the list, not open.

It can't stop what comes through an open door, and it never looks inside.A bad note through the guest door goes unnoticed — that's the note checker's job. Inside the walls nobody is checked — that's the castle that always asks.

In one breath

A firewall = the wall, plus the gatekeeper who opens and shuts each door by a list of who may use which door. Unlisted doors stay shut.

The oldest guard of all, around since the 1990s. Today's walls also carry the poster-watcher (IPS) and seal-breaking, and get called next-gen firewalls. iptables, pfSense, Palo Alto, Fortinet.

When grown-ups say it

Port
The door number. 443 is the guest door, 25 the mail door, 22 the admin door.
IP address
Where from. The messenger's home address. → the footprint
Rule
One line of the list. "Post office through the mail door → allow."
Default deny
Unlisted doors stay shut. Open only what's listed. The other way round is the castle with every door open.
Stateful
Remembering who went out. Replies get in; strangers don't.
Packet
The messenger. A little person carrying one note. The gatekeeper reads only the envelope.
Next-gen firewall
A wall with the poster-watcher inside. Adds wanted posters and seal-breaking — a peek past the envelope.
Internal / segmentation firewall
A wall inside the hallway. Splits the hallway inside too. → the hallway watcher
