In one breath

Virtual reality (VR) uses a headset to replace your whole field of view with a virtual world, making you feel like you're inside it.

VR uses a head-mounted display (HMD) to fully replace the user's view with virtual content, tracking head and hand movement to react in real time. It's distinct from AR, which overlays virtual information on an unobstructed view of reality, and MR, where reality and virtual content react to each other. Latency can trigger motion sickness ('cybersickness'), and extended-wear fatigue plus equipment cost remain barriers to wider adoption.

When grown-ups say it

Virtual Reality
A headset that replaces your whole view with virtual content. Covers your entire field of view.
Augmented Reality
Overlaying virtual info onto reality. Reality stays fully visible.
Mixed Reality
Reality and virtual content reacting to each other. Lets you handle virtual objects as if touching them.
Metaverse
The shared virtual space avatars gather in. VR is one way to experience it. → the Metaverse booth
HMD
The headset that fully covers your view. The core piece of VR hardware.
Cybersickness / VR Sickness
Dizziness caused by response delay. Worse the slower the tracking and response.
Head Tracking
Reading head direction to change the view. The key technology behind immersion.
Digital Twin
A twin that copies a real machine exactly. VR can even let you walk around inside a twin. → the Digital Twin booth
