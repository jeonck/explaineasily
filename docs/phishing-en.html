What is Phishing?

Phishing, told as a story about a letter that pretends to come from the post office.

Letters arrive at the castle every day.From the post office, the market, a friend… most of them are real.

The thief sends a letter dressed as the post office.'A parcel, today only, come here.' Urgent, scary, or too good to be true.

Phishing pretends to be someone you trust, and gets you to hand over the key.No wall is broken, no lock picked. It gets me to open the door myself.

It isn't only letters.By text (smishing), by phone (vishing), and aimed at you by name, boss and all (spear phishing).

Check the envelope, and pause at the door.Is the sender real, is the gate real. The ring stays dark at a fake gate. Still, one in a hundred falls for it — so there's a bell for reporting odd letters.

In one breath

Phishing = a letter pretending to be someone you trust, so you hand over the key or walk into a fake gate. It doesn't break the wall — it fools you.

Phishing is T1566 in the encyclopedia — the most common first step of a break-in. Defenses: training, a reporting bell, a ring that stays dark at fake gates (passkeys), and the real post office's stamp (SPF, DKIM, DMARC).

When grown-ups say it

Spear phishing
Aimed at one person. Written knowing your name, your boss, what you're working on.
Smishing
The text-message letter. 'Parcel arrived → link'. On a phone, you tap faster.
Vishing
The call. A voice pretending to be the post office, the bank, the police.
Phishing site
The look-alike door. Put the key in here and it goes to the thief. → the gatekeeper story
Sender address
Who it's really from. post-office.kr and post-offlce.kr — one letter apart.
SPF · DKIM · DMARC
The real post office's stamp. The directory records which post offices may send in my name.
Report button
The odd-letter bell. One ring and the guard room pulls the letter from everyone. → the guard room
