What are the Legal Procedures for Digital Evidence Collection?

In one breath

Legal digital evidence procedure = obtaining authorization (a warrant), keeping the scope limited, collecting it with the affected party present, and documenting the chain of custody — so it later counts as admissible evidence.

Separate from technical preservation (imaging, hashing), digital evidence collection must be carried out under lawful authority (such as a search warrant), limited to the minimum necessary scope, and with the affected party's right to be present respected. Violating these requirements can make even genuine evidence inadmissible in court under the exclusionary rule for unlawfully obtained evidence. Documenting an unbroken chain of custody is also a core requirement.

When grown-ups say it

Due Process
Properly following the set sequence of steps. Skip a step, and the result can be voided.
Admissibility of Evidence
Whether a court (or committee) will accept it as evidence at all. A separate question from whether the content is true.
Exclusionary Rule
The rule that improperly collected evidence can't be used, even if genuine. The core reason procedure matters so much.
Search Warrant
An official authorization, like the school's permission slip. Collect without it, and it's invalid from the start.
Right to Be Present
The right to witness collection, not have it happen in secret. Prevents later claims of secret tampering.
Chain of Custody
A record of who held it, and handed it to whom, when. Pairs with the technical side of setting evidence in place. → setting the footprint in plaster
Principle of Proportionality
Collecting only as much as needed, and no more. Balances privacy against the need to investigate.
