What is Public-Sector Cloud Adoption?

Public-sector cloud adoption (CSAP, guidelines, audit), told as a procurement rule: move in only to a certified building, into the grade your data earns.

Public agencies want in too, to save on cost.But it's public data — they can't just move in anywhere.

Move into just any building, and nobody can vouch for safety.An uncertified building is too risky for public data.

Move in only to a certified building, into the grade your data earns.This is how the public sector procures and adopts cloud.

In one breath

Public-sector cloud adoption means moving into a certified building (CSAP) only, into the grade that fits your data, through a set procedure.

To use private cloud, public agencies must choose a CSAP (Cloud Security Assurance Program)-certified service at the grade matching their data's classification (high/mid/low), contracted per the procurement agency's guide. After moving in, ongoing checks continue through IS audits and similar reviews. The CSAP certification criteria itself — the building's own safety-inspection checklist — is covered on a separate page.

When grown-ups say it

Public-sector cloud adoption
The rule: move into a certified building, at the right grade. Covers the procurement process and ongoing checks too.
CSAP
The building's own safety-inspection certification. This page focuses on the move-in/adoption side.
Data classification grade
Sensitivity split into high / mid / low. Higher grade, fewer qualifying spots.
Procurement guide
The procedure manual to follow before contracting. Keeps agencies from contracting on their own terms.
IS audit
The ongoing check even after move-in. Covered in depth in another PPE world. → the last checker before publication
Private vs government cloud
A building anyone can rent vs a certified one. Public data goes into the certified kind only.
Cloud service model
Bare shell / interior done / move-in ready. The grade rule applies no matter which model. → pick one to rent
Multi-cloud
The strategy of spreading across several buildings. Agencies sometimes use this for redundancy too. → moving into several buildings at once
