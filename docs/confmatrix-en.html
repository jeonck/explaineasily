What is a Confusion Matrix?

The confusion matrix, told as a story about a ledger that splits the sorter's calls against the real state into four boxes.

The sorter boasts 99% accuracy.Only 1 of 100 is rotten — stamping everything PASS still scores 99%.

Accuracy alone lets a rare mistake hide.Missing it every time still looks fine on paper.

A ledger splitting the real state against the call into four boxes.Not just one number — it shows exactly which kind of mistake happened.

TP — correctly passedreally PASS, called PASS
TN — correctly rejectedreally REJECT, called REJECT
FP — a false alarmflags a fine one as rejected
FN — a miss (the risky one)lets a rotten one through

Several metrics are computed straight from the four boxes.Which metric matters most depends on which kind of mistake costs more.

Which costs more, FN or FP, depends on the situation.Where the cutoff is set comes down to weighing these two costs against each other.

In one breath

Confusion matrix = a ledger splitting the real state against the sorter's call into four boxes (TP, TN, FP, FN).

A confusion matrix cross-tabulates a classifier's predictions against the ground truth into four boxes: TP (true positive), TN (true negative), FP (false positive), and FN (false negative). From these four numbers, metrics like accuracy, precision, recall, and the F1 score are derived. When a dataset is imbalanced, accuracy alone can't reveal a model's real performance — the confusion matrix has to be read alongside it.

When grown-ups say it

Confusion matrix
A ledger splitting the real state against the call into four boxes. Reveals the mistakes accuracy alone would hide.
Accuracy
Correct out of everything. Unreliable when the classes are imbalanced.
Precision
Of what it called PASS, how much really was. Matters most when false alarms are the costly mistake.
Recall
Of what's really PASS, how much it caught. Matters most when a miss is the costly mistake.
Imbalanced data
A situation where only 1 in 100 is rotten. Connects back to bias at the seed stage. → A Sorter Trained on Rotten Seed
Threshold
The cutoff line that splits PASS from REJECT. Moving this line trades off FP against FN.
False alarm vs. miss
A wrongful reject vs. letting a rotten one through. An adversarial attack ultimately shifts these same four numbers. → The Fake Dot Pattern Snuck Onto the Produce
ROC · AUC
The performance curve traced by sliding the threshold. Not one confusion matrix, but many of them strung together across thresholds.
