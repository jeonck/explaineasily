What is LOD & Open Data?

LOD (Linked Open Data) and open data, told as a story about a warehouse that opens its door and links its tags.

Nobody outside knows what's in this warehouse.The door is locked, and there's no label either.

Published, but no program can read it.Mismatched formats make reuse hard.

Open data/LOD opens the door, tags with a standard ID, and links it for anyone.Anyone can come and reuse it.

Open the doormake it visible to anyone
Organize as a tablegive it structure
Attach a standard tagnot locked to one program's format
Link to other warehouseslinked data becomes richer

Open data improves through these four steps.The last step is exactly LOD (Linked Open Data).

Openness doesn't guarantee quality.Openness fixes format, not the accuracy of the content.

In one breath

Open data/LOD opens the warehouse door to everyone, tags boxes with a standard ID (URI), and links them to other warehouses.

Open data is data published so anyone can freely access, reuse, and redistribute it. LOD (Linked Open Data), the top tier of Tim Berners-Lee's 5-star model, assigns URIs to data and links it to other data via RDF, forming a connected web of open data. Publishing it openly is a separate matter from its quality or freshness.

When grown-ups say it

Open Data
A warehouse opened for anyone to use. Real openness needs a clear license too.
Linked Open Data
Warehouses tagged and linked to each other. The highest tier of the 5-star model.
5-Star Open Data
Five steps a warehouse improves through. More stars, easier reuse.
URI
The standard tag attached to every box. Lets programs point to the same thing.
RDF
The rule for the string linking box to box. Written as subject–predicate–object.
NoSQL Modeling
A warehouse reshaping shelves to fit each box. Graph-type NoSQL fits linked data well. → the free-shelf warehouse
Normalization
Writing shared info on one shelf once. Open data is easier to manage with less duplication too. → the rule of writing it once
AI Privacy
Information that must be masked before publishing. Covered in depth on another page in the parrot world. → AI privacy
