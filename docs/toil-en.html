What is Toil?

Toil, told as a story about an operator who copies the exact same report by hand every morning.

Every morning, the operator copies the exact same report by hand.The same content as yesterday, copied by hand again today.

In one breath

Toil = repetitive, low-value manual work. Leave it unautomated, and it swallows all the time a person has.

A concept defined by Google SRE for operational work that's repetitive, automatable, and has little long-term value. Teams measure the toil ratio to manage it, and decide whether to automate by weighing the cost against the time it would save. The time freed up as toil shrinks often leads to bigger automation, like orchestration.

When grown-ups say it

Toil
Repetitive, low-value manual work. The more you automate, the less of it there is. → the people who keep the park open
Automation vs. manual
The line between what the machine does and what a person does. Repetition goes to the machine; judgment stays with the person.
Measuring toil
Timing how much time a task actually costs. Without measuring it, you can't even tell if it shrank.
Separating it from valuable work
Drawing a line against work that solves something new. Toil solves the exact same problem, over and over.
Deciding whether to invest in automation
Weighing the cost to build against the time it saves. Something rare enough might not be worth it.
Operational burden
The overall weight that builds up from accumulated toil. Pile it up, and there's no time left for anything new.
Orchestration
Once automation grows, coordinating many of them together. The next stage after automation starts cutting toil.
Runbook
The notebook where the manual steps were written down. Automation usually starts by turning this notebook into code.
