What is a Human Approval Gate?

A human approval gate, told as a story about a runner who waits for the owner's stamp before doing anything risky.

The runner is about to do something hard to undo, all on its own.Like paying or deleting. And the owner doesn't even know yet.

The runner doesn't stop by itself.It just confidently gets it done — it can't tell on its own that the job is hard to undo.

A human approval gate means a person makes the last check before a risky action runs.The runner stops and waits instead of just doing it. Only after the owner looks does the stamp come down.

Pause before it runsbefore it's done, not after
Only the hard-to-undo oneslike paying or deleting
Must approve to proceedonly moves once stamped
Reject it and it stays stoppedthe owner has the final word

Request, then wait, then a check, then approve or reject.Only ambiguous or hard-to-undo requests go through this line. Things like search or summarize just pass through.

Waiting for the owner every single time is slow.So when and for what this approval gate kicks in gets decided ahead of time, by the whole kitchen rules board.

In one breath

A human approval gate means the owner makes a final check and stamps it before the runner does anything hard to undo.

A human approval gate is a safeguard that pauses an autonomous agent right before it carries out a hard-to-undo or sensitive action — paying, deleting, sending something external — until a person explicitly approves it. It doesn't fix the underlying state of having too much authority; it's one concrete mechanism that inserts a person at the moment that authority is actually used.
