Human-in-the-loop, told as a story where the parrot prepares, a person looks and stamps, and only then does anything go out.

In one breath

Human-in-the-loop = the parrot prepares, a person looks and stamps, and only then does it go out. Put it on what is hard to undo (sending, paying, deleting), let the parrot ask when unsure, and send only the risky ones to a person — a stamp without a look is no fence.

A workflow design where a person approves, edits, or rejects model output before it takes effect. Put an approval step on hard-to-undo actions (sending, paying, deleting), escalate when confidence falls below a threshold, and keep approvals and edits as an audit log and fine-tuning data. Tier the level of automation by risk, and keep review load low enough that approval never becomes rubber-stamping.

When grown-ups say it

Human-in-the-loop
A person at the end. The parrot prepares, a person looks and stamps, then it goes out.
Approval step
Where the stamp goes. Before sending, paying, deleting. The harder to undo, the more it matters.
Confidence threshold
The line where the parrot asks first. Below this much confidence, it goes to a person: please check this one.
Escalation
Handing it up to a person. What the parrot cannot decide goes to a person; what they cannot, to someone above.
Audit log
The notebook of who stamped what, when. When something goes wrong, you trace it back here. → the security world's logbook
Level of automation
How much the parrot does alone. Greetings alone, refunds after a stamp, deletions by a person.
Guardrail
The automatic fence before the person. A machine filters first; a person looks at what is left. → the fence
Agent
The parrot that plans its own errands. Decide where along the errand the stamps go. → the agent
