In one breath

LLMDoS = an attack that keeps the kitchen's stove burning with just a few tickets until it stalls. Instead of stacking trucks, it exhausts the kitchen by demanding costly computation over and over.

LLMDoS (LLM Denial of Service) is an attack that exhausts a model's compute, memory, or time budget by sending long, repetitive, or recursively expanding requests. Unlike traditional DDoS, which pushes with sheer network traffic volume, LLMDoS can degrade or stall a service with only a handful of requests by triggering expensive token generation or long-context processing. Token/time limits, per-request cost estimation, and fair queuing are the main defenses.

When grown-ups say it

LLMDoS
The order that keeps the stove burning. Just a few tickets can stall the whole kitchen.
Compute-bomb ticket
A short note that demands a mountain of work. A recursively expanding request is the classic shape of one.
vs. DDoS
Stacking trucks at the door. The general attack that floods network traffic. → the security world's DDoS
Rate limiting
Lining up incoming tickets. It has to gate on cost, not count, to stop LLMDoS. → the theme park's rate limiting
Availability
Guests getting served in time. It's the first thing to break when the stove stalls. → the theme park's reliability
Token limit
The max number of beans usable at once. It caps the size of the ticket itself ahead of time. → the parrot world's beans
Cost-based throttling
A fence that measures work, not just count. Even the same number of tickets can carry wildly different costs.
Capacity planning
Figuring out ahead of time how many stoves can run at once. You have to size for both normal and peak load ahead of time. → the theme park's capacity planning
