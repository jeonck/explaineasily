What is DevOps?

DevOps, told as a story about tearing down the wall between the writer and the printer, and trading small batches often instead.

The writer throws the manuscript over the wall.Throw it over the wall, and problems only surface on the other side.

When responsibility is split, problems get caught too late.Each watching only their own part — problems surface only at the very end.

DevOps tears down the wall and prints together in small, frequent runs.Writer and printer stand side by side, with no wall, trading small batches often.

In one breath

DevOps = tearing down the wall between the writer (dev) and the printer (ops), and printing together in small, frequent, automated batches.

DevOps is a culture and set of practices that bring development (Dev) and operations (Ops) together as one team instead of separate silos, shipping small changes often through an automated pipeline of continuous integration (CI) and continuous delivery (CD). Success is measured with metrics like deployment frequency, change lead time, change failure rate, and recovery time.

When grown-ups say it

DevOps
The writer and the printer, with no wall between them. Development and operations become one team.
Continuous Integration
Merging manuscript changes often, in small pieces. Every merge gets checked automatically.
Continuous Delivery/Deployment
Automatically feeding a checked manuscript onto the print line. No one has to push it through by hand each time.
Zero-Downtime Deployment
Swapping in a new edition without closing the bookstore. Specific methods include blue-green and canary → running twin rides, one at a time, a quiet test run in the corner.
Infrastructure as Code
Writing down the print shop's own setup as text, too. The equipment itself can be version-controlled like a manuscript → the blueprint-as-code in the theme-park world.
Shared Responsibility Culture
The attitude of fixing problems together, not pointing fingers. Puts 'let's fix it together' ahead of 'whose fault is it'.
DORA Metrics
The four rulers that measure whether this is working. Deployment frequency, lead time, failure rate, and recovery time → the four performance metrics.
DevSecOps
Bringing a guard into the wall-free space too. The broader version that brings security in from the start, too → the builder and the guard, side by side.
