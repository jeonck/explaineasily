What is TCP/UDP?

TCP and UDP, told as a story about a registered envelope that confirms delivery and a plain one that just flies.

A sure delivery for papers, a fast one for video.Different goals need different ways of promising delivery.

Confirming is slow; skipping it risks losing things.Three greetings are slow; no confirmation at all is risky.

A registered envelope is TCP; a plain one is UDP.Both use the same road — they just promise differently.

3-way handshakethree greetings before anything is sent
Sequence numbernumbers let pieces be put back in order
Checksuma stamp on the outside checks for damage
Port numberwrites exactly which room number to deliver to

A TCP header carries more fields.Both have ports, but only TCP has sequencing, handshake, and a kept-open connection.

Neither is the one right answer.Registered suits papers and payments; plain suits video and games.

In one breath

TCP is the registered envelope that confirms and keeps order; UDP is the plain one that goes fast, with no confirmation.

TCP is a connection-oriented protocol: it establishes a connection via a 3-way handshake, then guarantees reliability through sequence numbers, ACKs, and retransmission. UDP skips connection setup entirely, keeping its header light and fast — used where a little loss is acceptable, like real-time streaming or DNS queries.

When grown-ups say it

TCP
The registered envelope that confirms and keeps order. Trades speed for reliability.
UDP
The plain envelope that just goes fast. Light and fast, but can lose a piece.
3-way handshake
The three greetings exchanged before anything is sent. Only TCP goes through this step.
Port number
The exact room number inside the house. The dedicated hole that says exactly who gets it. → the dedicated mailbox hole
Error/flow/congestion control
The three devices that keep a registered delivery safe to the end. The fine-grained machinery TCP uses. → the three safety devices
Checksum
The confirming stamp on the envelope's outside. A quick check for whether the contents got damaged.
DNS
The neighborhood address book. A classic example of a fast UDP question-and-answer. → the neighborhood address book
Socket
A dedicated hole made from an address plus a room number. Whichever envelope you use, it all goes through this hole. → the dedicated mailbox hole
