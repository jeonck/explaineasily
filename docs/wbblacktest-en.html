What is White-box/Black-box Testing?

White-box and black-box testing, told through whether the proofreader opens up the manuscript's inner flow, or just reads the finished book like a reader.

It looked fine on the surface, so it got passed.An error hidden somewhere inside went unseen.

Opinions split on whether to look from inside or outside.Insisting on only one way misses the other kind of error.

White-box opens it up to look inside; black-box just reads from the outside.Both are done by the proofreader — just from different vantage points.

Testing isn't proof there are no bugs.It's only proof that the bugs it found are real.

In one breath

White-box/black-box testing = whether you check by looking at the manuscript's inner flow, or only at the finished book from outside.

White-box testing verifies code by knowing its internal structure (branches, loops) and tracing those paths, while black-box testing verifies functionality using only input and output, without knowing the internals. Boundary value analysis and equivalence partitioning are classic black-box techniques; statement, decision, and condition coverage are classic white-box ones.

When grown-ups say it

White-box Testing
Proofing by looking at the manuscript's inner flow. Traces the code's branches and paths.
Black-box Testing
Proofing only the finished book. Checks using only input and output.
Boundary Value Analysis
Watching the start and end points closely. Errors often show up right at the edge.
Equivalence Partitioning
Grouping similar cases and checking one. Because you can't check every single case.
Unit/Integration/System Testing
Checks scoped from one chapter up to the whole book. The wider the scope, the later it happens.
Requirements Engineering
Deciding beforehand what counts as correct. The test's own standard ultimately comes from the spec. → the one who asks before writing
Regression Testing
Rechecking the old parts after a fix. A fix can break something else.
Coverage
The share of the manuscript's paths actually walked. Even 100% doesn't mean there are no bugs.
