What is a CNN (Convolutional Neural Network)?

CNNs, told as a story about an eye that scans a small window bit by bit, instead of staring at the whole photo at once.

Trying to take in the whole photo at once — there are too many numbers.Looking at every single pixel separately is simply too much.

Looking only at loose numbers, local patterns slip past.You have to keep track of position to ever see the pattern.

A small window slides bit by bit, finding local patterns.Stacking up what it finds this way, it eventually understands the whole picture.

In one breath

A CNN = a neural network that scans a photo through a small window, bit by bit, finding local patterns and stacking them into the whole picture.

A CNN (Convolutional Neural Network) is built around the convolution operation: sliding a small filter (kernel) across the whole input to find local patterns — edges, color patches, and the like. Stacking convolution, activation, and pooling across many layers lets shallow layers recognize simple patterns while deeper layers recognize more complex shapes. CNNs are especially strong at image and video classification, but because they rely on pixel-level patterns, they remain vulnerable to subtle perturbations like adversarial examples.

When grown-ups say it

CNN
The eye that scans through a small window, bit by bit. Built around the convolution operation.
Convolution
The operation of sliding a small filter bit by bit. The core move that hunts down local patterns.
Filter (kernel)
The small dashed window that hunts for a pattern. Many different filters exist, each hunting a different kind of pattern.
Feature map
The small map where only pattern-found spots stay bright. What comes out after one pass of convolution.
Pooling
Shrinking the map, keeping only the strongest signal. Cuts computation and makes it less rattled by small shifts in position.
Layer
The stage where small patterns build into bigger ones. Shallow layers see simply; deep layers see complexity.
Fully connected layer
The layer that gathers everything stacked up and makes the final call. Sits at the very end of a CNN, deciding the bin.
