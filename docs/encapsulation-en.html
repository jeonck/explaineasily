What is Encapsulation?

Encapsulation and information hiding, told as a story about a writer who hides draft notes in a drawer and only hands finished chapters through a submission box.

The editor reaches straight into the writer's drawer and edits the notes.The inside of the drawer was supposed to be the writer's alone.

With everyone reaching into each other's drawers, nothing lines up anymore.When anyone can touch the inside, everything gets tangled.

Encapsulation hides the drawer's inside and hands things over only through a fixed box.Change the inside, and if the box's shape stays the same, nobody outside notices.

Hide the insideonly the writer opens the drawer
Expose only through the boxonly the finished piece passes through

What's inside the locked drawer differs from what the submission box shows.Change the inside, and nobody notices as long as the box's shape stays the same.

Encapsulation doesn't catch a mistake in the design itself.Encapsulation hides the inside — it doesn't guarantee the design itself is right.

In one breath

Encapsulation (information hiding) bundles data and behavior together, hides the inside, and exposes only a fixed interface to the outside.

Encapsulation bundles an object's data and methods together and exposes only a public interface, hiding the internal implementation — a core OOP principle. Because the private internals can change freely as long as the external interface stays the same, it makes code resilient to change and prevents unintended direct manipulation.

When grown-ups say it

Encapsulation
Bundling data and behavior together. Hides the inside, exposes only the box.
Information Hiding
Locking the drawer. The principle that makes encapsulation possible.
Public Interface
The submission box. The only way in or out from the outside.
Private Field
Notes inside the locked drawer. The outside can't touch it directly.
Getter/Setter
The clerk's hand that fetches it for you. Instead of opening the drawer, it hands things over only the agreed way.
Modularity
Giving every desk its own drawer. Stack enough encapsulation and you get modularity.
Refactoring
Tidying up the notes inside the drawer. The box stays the same while the inside gets tidied. → keep the outside, retouch the inside
Unit Test
Checking by feeding things straight into the box. You can verify it by watching just the public box.
