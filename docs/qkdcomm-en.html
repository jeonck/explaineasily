In one breath

Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) = exchanging a key made of particles of light. Peeking changes the light's state, so eavesdropping is caught instantly.

QKD distributes cryptographic keys using the quantum-mechanical property that observation disturbs a system's state. Unlike public-key encryption, which relies on computational hardness and may be vulnerable to future quantum computers, QKD detects eavesdropping through physics itself. QKD only distributes the key; the message itself is still encrypted with symmetric encryption. Distance limits and high deployment costs remain obstacles to wide use. It is a separate technology from PQC (post-quantum cryptography), which instead uses math-based algorithms designed to resist quantum computers.

When grown-ups say it

Quantum Key Distribution
Sharing a key made of light. Catches eavesdropping through the physical law that observation disturbs the state.
Photon
A particle of light. The unit that carries each bit of the key.
Quantum computer threat
The far stronger future computer. Could someday crack some of today's encryption through sheer computation.
Post-Quantum Cryptography
A different approach — holding out with math. Unlike QKD, it uses new math-based algorithms to prepare for future computers.
Eavesdropping detection
A peek is caught instantly. A feature unique to QKD — the physics itself gives it away.
Key distribution
Delivering only the key. The letter itself is still locked with ordinary encryption. → the letter nobody can read without the key
Distance limits, relays
Light can't travel far. Needs relay stations, which means more equipment.
Deployment cost
Still expensive. That's why it's used only on a few critical stretches, not across all communications.
