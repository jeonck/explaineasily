What is a Hallucination?

AI hallucination, told as a story about a parrot that continues confidently and plausibly even when it doesn't know.

A guest asks who wrote a paper, and the parrot gives a name, a year, even a title.Very confidently, very plausibly. But that paper doesn't exist.

In its books, a paper was always followed by a name and a year. So it continues in that shape.It has almost never picked the I-don't-know bean, so that one sits at the bottom. The parrot continues the shape, not the meaning.

A hallucination is the parrot continuing plausibly even when it doesn't know.The mountain-of-books parrot was born this way. Doubt names, numbers and dates most, and it errs far less when a real page the librarian found sits on its tray.

It can't be removed completely — continuing is the bird's nature.So the answer is a draft and a person checks it. In front of risky jobs like money or medicine, put up a fence — the thief's parrot story lives on the security side.

In one breath

Hallucination = the parrot continuing confidently and plausibly even when it doesn't know. Doubt names, numbers, dates; ask for sources; put the librarian's real page on the tray. It never fully goes away — the answer is a draft, a person checks.

A language model generates content that isn't true because it keeps producing the next token that looks plausible under its training distribution. Prompt instructions, RAG (injecting retrieved evidence), fine-tuning and low temperature reduce it but never remove it. Source citation, factuality evaluation, calibration (matching confidence to accuracy) and guardrails for high-risk tasks are used alongside.

When grown-ups say it

Hallucination
The plausible lie. Comes from continuing what it doesn't know. Not a mistake so much as a make-up.
Grounding
Speaking from real text. Put the page the librarian found on the tray, and it continues from that.
Retrieval-augmented generation
Librarian + parrot. For every question the librarian searches first, then the parrot answers from that page. The easiest way to add new knowledge.
Source citation
Saying where it saw it. An answer with a source can be checked by a person. No source means it's a draft.
Temperature
The randomness dial. Turn it down and it picks only the obvious bean. Less randomness, but confident errors stay.
Factuality evaluation
Checking answers against a key. An examiner measures how often it makes things up, using hundreds of questions with known answers.
Calibration
Is it right as often as it sounds sure? A well-calibrated parrot speaks softly when unsure. The plausible parrot is always loud.
Prompt
The trainer's note. Say so if you do not know — that one line helps a little. → the trainer's note
