What is an Outlier?

Outliers, told as a story about spotting the one piece of produce that stands out from a group of otherwise similar ones.

Among similarly sized apples, one stands out — much bigger.Even at a glance, it's clearly different from the rest.

Ignore it and the numbers skew; discard it and a signal might be lost.An outlier might be a mistake — or it might be a genuinely important signal.

A data point that's statistically, clearly unlike the rest of the group.'Different' isn't a feeling — it's measured by distance or spread.

Look at distancehow far from the group's center
Look at standard deviationhow many steps from average (Z-score)
Look at quartilesoutside the middle bulk of the data (IQR)
Check the reason firstnot just tossing it out automatically

Different methods — but they all come down to agreeing on a cutoff line.Where that line is drawn decides whether the very same item counts as an outlier or not.

Not every outlier is meant to be thrown away.An item tampered with by an adversarial attack can also look statistically like an outlier.

In one breath

An outlier = a data point that, measured by distance or standard deviation, is clearly unlike the rest of the group.

An outlier is a value clearly unlike the rest of the data, found using statistical criteria like distance from a cluster's center, Z-score (standard deviations from the mean), or being outside the IQR (interquartile range). It can be the trace of a sensor error or a data-entry mistake — or it can be a rare but genuinely important signal, like a fraudulent transaction, a rare disease, or the trace of an attack. The rule of thumb is to investigate the cause before deleting it, not the other way around. The 'noise' that DBSCAN clustering sets aside is itself one face of an outlier.

When grown-ups say it

Outlier
The piece of produce that stands out from the crowd. Found by measuring distance or standard deviation.
Z-score
The number of standard deviations a value sits from the mean. An absolute value past 3 is commonly treated as an outlier.
IQR (interquartile range)
The range where the middle 50% of the data sits. Falling far outside this range marks something as an outlier.
Noise
