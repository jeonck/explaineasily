In one breath

ISO/IEC 25000 (SQuaRE) = a shared checklist that defines "good software" with 8 quality characteristics, then measures, requires, and evaluates it.

The ISO/IEC 25000 series (SQuaRE — Systems and Software Quality Requirements and Evaluation) is a family of international standards that define, measure, require, and evaluate software quality. The 2501n quality model defines eight characteristics — functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability — while 2502n (measurement), 2503n (requirements), and 2504n (evaluation) provide the process to quantify, compare, and judge them.

When grown-ups say it

SQuaRE
The standard series for good software. The umbrella name for the ISO/IEC 25000-series standards.
Quality Model (ISO 25010)
The list defining the eight quality characteristics. Defines function, performance, usability, and the rest, one by one.
Quality Measurement (ISO 2502n)
How to measure each trait in numbers. Uses measurable indicators, not just a feeling.
Quality Requirements (ISO 2503n)
The target level written down in advance. Becomes the baseline for evaluation.
Quality Evaluation (ISO 2504n)
The process of comparing target against actual and judging. Falling short means it has to be fixed again.
Usability
How easy something is to use. One of the eight quality characteristics.
Maintainability
How easy it is to fix later. Matters more the longer software stays in use.
Software Testing
Actually running it to check. The process that actually checks SQuaRE's standard. → the last checker before publication
