In one breath

QKD (Quantum Key Distribution) sends an encryption key riding on single photons of light, so that any eavesdropping attempt is always caught thanks to quantum properties.

QKD is a key-distribution technology that exploits quantum mechanics' observation-disturbance principle: any eavesdropping changes a photon's quantum state, letting both sender and receiver detect it. A key found to be eavesdropped on is discarded, and only a verified-safe key is used for encryption. However, it's limited by optical-fiber distance and needs expensive dedicated equipment, and QKD itself handles only 'safe key distribution' — actual data encryption still needs a separate method, like a symmetric-key algorithm.

When grown-ups say it

QKD
A light-based key exchange that always gets caught when peeked at. Relies on the quantum properties of photons.
Quantum Entanglement/Superposition
The quantum property where observing changes the state. The principle behind QKD's eavesdropping detection.
Symmetric-Key Encryption
The algorithm that actually encrypts the data. Uses the key QKD has safely shared.
Quantum Computer
Future tech that might crack today's encryption. QKD is designed to stay safe even against this.
Blockchain Tech & Security
A different way of blocking tampering, via hashing and consensus. Both protect trust, but in different ways. → the Blockchain Tech & Security booth
Photon
The particle of light that carries one piece of the key. QKD's smallest unit of transmission.
Fiber Distance Limit
Why QKD can't yet travel very far. One of the obstacles to wider use.
Private Key
What QKD safely shares. QKD can't help if this is lost.
